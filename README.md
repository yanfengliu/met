# met

A procedural three.js model of the Metropolitan Museum of Art's Fifth Avenue building in New York, outside and inside.

Live: https://yanfengliu.github.io/met/

The page does not load a finished model. It assembles one from an ordered list of build steps, in the order the real building grew, and shows the finished building. You can orbit it, cut it open, look at one floor at a time and walk through it in first person.

Every part is generated by code. There are no imported meshes, scans or photo textures, and the page fetches no assets from the network.

## What it shows

The steps fall in nine chapters, from the bare site to the 2014 plaza, and each part belongs to the era it was built in. Only parts that exist today are modelled. The Wallace Wing and its roof garden, closed in 2025, stand as they did until then, and the step text says the Tang Wing is replacing them.

Inside, it models the major public spaces: the Great Hall and Grand Staircase, the Temple of Dendur gallery, the American Wing's Charles Engelhard Court, the Greek and Roman court, the Arms and Armor court, the Medieval Sculpture Hall, the Lehman Wing court, the Petrie Court, the Astor Court and the skylights of the European Paintings galleries. It does not model every gallery. In ten spaces, 94 placeholders stand for real objects on view there: plain forms at each object's real size, coloured from The Met's open-access collection data, each with a card that gives its title, maker, date and a link to its page (see Artwork cards). The three doorways of the Fifth Avenue entrance and Weston's 1888 door in the Petrie Court open and close.

## Run it

You need Node 24 (see `.nvmrc`), npm and a browser with WebGL 2.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

The dev server listens on `http://127.0.0.1:5310/`. Set `MET_PORT` to a whole number from 1024 to 65535 to use another port. The server fails instead of moving to the next port when its port is taken, so a client never reaches another worktree's server.

The page opens on the finished building. Press F, or the Walk button, to walk.

`npm run build` writes both pages to `dist/` with relative URLs, so the built site can be served from any path. `npm run preview` serves `dist/` on the same port.

## Controls

The Walk button at the bottom right switches walk mode on or off (see Walk mode below). The View menu at the top right holds the camera presets, the walk spawns and the section cut, and the floor picker sits at the left.

### Keyboard

The keys work in two modes. W, A, S, D, Q, E and F are physical keys, so they sit in the same place on any keyboard layout.

| Key | Orbit view | Walk mode |
|---|---|---|
| Space | Nothing | Jump |
| Arrows | Nothing | Walk forward, back, left, right |
| Page Up, Page Down | Go up, down a floor (see Floor picker) | Nothing |
| W, S | Move the camera forward, back over the ground | Walk forward, back |
| A, D | Move the camera left, right | Walk left, right |
| Q | Move the camera down | Nothing |
| E | Move the camera up | Open or close the door you look at |
| Shift, held | Move twice as fast | Run |
| F | Walk | Back to the orbit view |
| Esc | Close an open menu or the artwork card | Back to the orbit view |

In the orbit view, W, A, S, D, Q and E move the camera and the point it orbits together. They move faster the farther the camera is from that point. A focused button keeps Space and Enter for itself, a focused slider keeps its arrow keys, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down, and a text field keeps every key.

### View menu

- **Exterior** and **Interior**: camera presets. The ids for `view=` are in the tables below. The page opens on `aerial-ne`; a `view=` that names no preset is ignored.
- **Walk**: walk from the plaza, the Great Hall or the top of the Grand Staircase.
- **Section cut**: Cut switches a horizontal cut on or off, and the slider sets its height in metres, in 0.1 m steps up to just above the model's top. Moving the slider switches the cut on. Only the building is cut: everything of it above the cut is removed, shadows included, and cut walls and slabs are drawn solid in one flat colour, as in an architect's section, while everything the site builds as landscape (trees, ground, streets, paths, rocks, walls, fences, lamps, benches, fountains and the plaza's furniture) stays whole. Use it, the floor picker or an interior preset to see inside.

### Floor picker

The column of buttons at the left of the view shows one floor at a time: All at the top, then Roof (the roof garden), Second, Main and Ground. Page Up and Page Down move up and down it, with All above the top floor.

- Choosing a floor sets the section cut to just under the slab over it, so its walls show cut like a plan: Ground at 3.1 m, Main at 7.8 m, Second at 12.7 m, and the roof garden at 27.4 m, over its own tallest piece. The heights were picked by looking at the model, and `src/viewer/floors.ts` says why for each.
- Trees, lamps and the rest of the landscape that rise above the cut are hidden whole rather than cut, since the cut leaves landscape alone. The ground itself always stays.
- The camera frames the floor from above at 65°, from the side you were looking from.
- All gives back the cut you had before, on or off, and shows the landscape again. Moving the cut slider or pressing Cut by hand leaves the floor view instead, and your cut stands.
- Walk mode leaves the floor view too, and the picker hides while you walk. What you collide with never changes.
- No entry shows the map's floor-3 plates or the mezzanines. Where a wing's second floor lies higher than the Great Hall's balcony (the Fifth Avenue links, the American Wing), the Second view shows that wing's floor below.

### Camera

Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, and use the wheel, a pinch or a middle-button drag to zoom toward the pointer. Each wheel notch moves the camera a tenth of the way to the surface under the pointer, and at least 0.25 m. Zooming in stops 0.5 m before that surface, and over open sky it keeps flying forward. Zooming out stops when the camera is 6 km from the point it orbits. The camera stays above the ground.

### Walk mode

Press F or the Walk button, or choose a place under Walk in the View menu, to stand in the model in first person. The pointer locks to the view, and the mouse looks around as it moves, with no button held. W, A, S, D or the arrow keys walk, Shift runs and Space jumps. Esc, F or the Walk button goes back to the orbit view, with the camera where you stood. So does anything else that frees the pointer, such as switching to another window. F then walks on from the same place. A page opened with `walk=1` leaves the pointer free until you click the view. Where the browser gives no lock, and on a touch screen, drag to look. Walking needs a keyboard: a touch screen can look around but not move.

While you walk, a crosshair marks the middle of the view. While the pointer is locked the mouse reaches no button: use Tab and Enter, or leave walk mode with Esc.

Walls, columns and closed doors stop you, and you slide along them. You climb stairs and the front steps just by walking, since you step up edges of up to 0.35 m. You can walk up slopes of up to 40°, steeper ones stop you, and you fall off ledges. You walk through water and foliage. Neither the section cut nor a floor view changes what you collide with, and entering walk mode leaves a floor view. Entering walk mode first builds the collision for the whole building, which takes under 2 seconds.

### Doors

Look at a door within 2 m while walking, and the crosshair turns gold, with E Open, E Close or Locked under it. E, or a click while the pointer is locked, swings the door open away from you, or shut. The three doorways of the Fifth Avenue entrance open either way, and Weston's door in the Petrie Court opens into the building only. The doors of the McKim, Mead & White links and the bank door in the American Wing court stay locked. A closed door stops you, and a door never shuts on you: one that would waits until you step clear. `doors=open` in the URL opens every door that opens at load.

### Artwork cards

Look at an artwork within 6 m while walking, or click it in the orbit view, and a card at the bottom left gives its title, maker, date, medium, gallery and size, with any measurement the record lacks marked as estimated. It ends with a link to the object's page on metmuseum.org, which opens in a new tab, and the credit for The Met's data. While the pointer is locked, the card says "Press Esc, then click the link": Esc leaves walk mode and keeps the card. In the orbit view, Esc, a click on anything else or a camera preset hides it. The page itself asks nothing of metmuseum.org.

## URL parameters

`src/viewer/query.ts` is the only reader of the page URL. A malformed value is ignored, with a console warning that names the parameter and what would be accepted; a parameter it does not know is ignored.

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| `view=<preset id>` | Open on that camera preset. |
| `cam=px,py,pz,tx,ty,tz` | Open with this camera position and target, in metres. |
| `cut=<metres>` | Switch the section cut on at this height. |
| `solo=<chapter id>` | Build only that chapter, plus `site`. |
| `walk=1` | Start in walk mode. |
| `spawn=<id>` or `spawn=x,y,z,tx,ty,tz` | With `walk=1`, where to start walking: `plaza` (the default), `great-hall` or `second-floor`, or with your feet at (x, y, z), in metres, looking at (tx, ty, tz). |
| `doors=open` or `doors=closed` | The state every door that opens takes at load; closed is the default. Locked doors stay shut. |
| `floor=<id>` | Open on that floor of the floor picker: `ground`, `main`, `second` or `roof-garden` (`all` shows every floor). It is framed unless `view=` or `cam=` names a camera, and it is ignored with `walk=1`. |
| `noui=1` | Hide the page chrome. |
| `shot=1` | Capture mode: no animation. `window.__shotReady` becomes true once at least 5 frames have rendered at the requested state. |

Coordinates are in the world frame: metres, +Y up, +X up Fifth Avenue (Manhattan north), +Z toward Fifth Avenue. The origin is at sidewalk grade on the central entrance axis, in the plane of the Hunt facade's main wall face.

`window.__met` exposes read-only facts for tests and capture scripts: step ids, chapters, presets, floors, camera, cut and floor view, the doors, the artwork card in view, budget statistics, build timings, and the walk mode's state (the eye, the look direction, the ground contact, the pointer lock, the look target and the collision build time). Tests drive the page through its real controls and read state from it.

## Parts gallery

`http://127.0.0.1:5310/parts.html` shows every part of the parametric library in `src/model/lib/` on a labelled grid: arches, balustrades, domes and vaults, Egyptian parts, fittings, glazing, landscape, mouldings, orders (columns, pilasters, capitals), stairs, walls and windows.

| Parameter | Effect |
|---|---|
| `part=<id>` | Show one exhibit alone and frame it, for example `part=column-corinthian`. |
| `view=<name>` | The angle: `three-quarter` (default), `front`, `side`, `back`, `rear-quarter`, `top`, `low` or `up`. |
| `closeup=<name>` | Frame one of the exhibit's named close-up boxes. |
| `shot=1` | Capture mode, as on the main page. |
| `noui=1` | Hide the labels. |

## Checks

`npm run verify` is the pre-commit gate: unit tests, type check, lint, build and the Playwright end-to-end suite on the installed Chrome. What each step covers, the capture commands (`npm run shots`, `npm run sweep`) and the dependency audits are in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), under Gates.

## Where things are

- `src/build/`: the build engine: step types, batching into instanced meshes, the timeline that builds the model from them, the chapter list and the composed sequence.
- `src/model/`: the model. `frame.ts` holds the world-frame constants, each with its source, `palette.ts` the shared materials, `lib/` the parametric parts, and `site`, `core`, `hunt`, `mmw`, `roche-north` and `roche-south` the six modelling areas. `collection/` holds the 94 artworks: the picks, the catalogue generated from them and the placeholders' forms.
- `src/viewer/`: renderer, camera, zoom, presets, section cut, floors and the floor view, the doors, the artwork labels, and URL parsing. `src/walk/`: the walk mode, its walker, its collision and the look target. `src/ui/`: the View menu, the Walk button, floor picker, keys and the artwork card. `src/parts/`: the gallery page.
- `scripts/`: capture tooling, and `met-catalogue.ts`, which regenerates the artworks' catalogue from The Met's Collection API when the picks change (`npm run met-catalogue`, run by hand, never in the build). `tests/e2e/`: Playwright tests.
- `docs/design/spec.md`: the contract for the world frame, the build-step API, the page and the budgets.
- `docs/research/`: the research behind the model. `exterior.md` has the footprint, the blocks and the dimensions, `interior.md` the interior spaces, `history.md` the chronology and the chapter list, `collection.md` how the artworks' catalogue is made, and `exterior-images.md` and `interior-images.md` the reference photographs.
- `docs/work/`: each work unit's plan and review rounds. `docs/devlog/`: the history. `docs/learning/`: the lessons, the gates' red proofs and the defect register. `docs/policies/local-rules.md`: the rules that bind only this repository.

## Reference photographs

The model is checked against photographs of the real building, but the photographs are not in this repository and are never committed. `docs/research/exterior-images.md` and `docs/research/interior-images.md` list each one with its URL, author, licence, what it shows and where the camera stood. Local copies, renders and captures live only under ignored paths: `.shots/`, `output/`, `tmp/` and `research/cache/`.
