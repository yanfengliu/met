# Defect register

Defects the owner reported, each with the symptom as they saw it, what the investigation found, the cause, and the check that covers the defect's class from now on. An entry stays after its gate lands: this is the list of what the gates could not see, which is where the next defect comes from.

## The Pages build times out a unit test (2026-10-28)

**Reported symptom:** Relayed at the owner's request by the session closing work unit 4: "main's CI is red: one test in src/model/closure.test.ts times out on the GitHub runner, so Pages is not deploying." The test, "stand every upright face of a solid by its inside: a part with no top and no bottom has none and reads as outside", failed with "Test timed out in 5000ms" on `1413777` (run 40726497630) and `bfb7699` (run 40746772742), and the deploy job was skipped.

**Investigation:** The job logs of the three Pages runs since the test landed list every test over 300 ms. The test took 3157 ms at `a4af999`, which passed, 5631 ms at `1413777` and 6391 ms at `bfb7699`. Two runs of the whole suite on the local machine took 2265 and 2784 ms for it. So the runner ran it up to 2.8 times slower than the local machine, and the crossing query and the Dendur pool walk up to 2.3 times (3256 against 1427 ms, 3956 against 1752 ms). The runner's own speed varied about twice over between runs: 3157 to 6391 ms for this test, and 22.4 to 41.5 s for `src/model/reach.test.ts`. On the runs that failed, the pool walk took up to 79% of the 5 s default and the crossing query up to 65%.

**Cause:** Every unit test but the repo conventions' reads (30 s) ran on Vitest's 5 s default, and nothing compared a test's time with its timeout on the local machine. The upright-face test came in with work unit 6 (`dc1a7dc`) and took under half the default here, so `npm run verify` passed. The Pages run at `a4af999` passed at 3.2 s, and `1413777` changed docs only, so its run was not waited on (R9); that run was the first to fail.

**Correction:** Every test now has 60 s (`vitest.config.ts`), the repo conventions' reads included. The runner's slowest test, 6.4 s, has nine times that.

**Check:** `scripts/test-headroom.ts`, a Vitest reporter that `vitest.config.ts` adds off GitHub's runner (`CI` not `true`), fails the run when a passed test takes over a sixth of its timeout, or when Vitest gives no time or timeout for one. It names each test with its time, its timeout and a timeout that would pass it. A sixth leaves room for both measurements: a test within it here takes under half its timeout on the slowest run measured there. It covers the class "a unit test passes here and times out on GitHub's runner" within its bound: each test's own time, measured where it runs, against the ratio measured on 2026-10-28. It reads no hook's or module collection's time: `src/model/reach.test.ts` fills in a `beforeAll` under its own 300 s, and the file took up to 41.5 s there. `scripts/test-headroom.test.ts` holds the rule.

**Gate proof:** With Vitest's 5 s default back (no `testTimeout`, the conventions' 30 s kept), `npx vitest run` exited 1 and named 6 of 650 tests: the upright faces (2784 ms), the Dendur pool walk (1863), the crossing query (1609), random history 11 (1335), the Grand Staircase built under a walker (1053) and the window parts (853). With `CI=true` the same run exited 0 with no report. With the 60 s timeout it exits 0.

**What it says about other checks:** A bound that only the slower machine enforces fails after every local gate has passed, so a check of time on the local machine needs headroom measured on the slower one. A docs-only push is not waited on (R9), yet its run tests the code again: `1413777`'s red run stood unseen until the next push failed the same way.

## The Weston door's head is round (2026-10-28)

**Reported symptom:** "the 1888 Weston entrance door into the Petrie Court may have the wrong head shape": the model built it arched, with a voussoir ring and a fanlight, and work unit 2's doors worker had noted that photograph #33 shows it square-headed.

**Investigation:** Every source in `docs/research/` that shows the door, each read at its native size (`docs/research/interior.md`, Weston's 1888 door): the court photographs #33 (6000 × 4000) and #35 and Heckscher's fig. 110 (1995); Weston's 1883 study, his 1888 plan, his approved elevation of 1885 and the porch's drawing of April 1888 (Heckscher figs. 26 to 29); the King's Handbook engraving of 1891; and, too oblique to read the head, Heckscher's fig. 35 (about 1895) and the Detroit Publishing photograph of about 1900. Every one that shows the head shows it square, in a moulded architrave, with no fanlight. The 1888 drawing, at a quarter inch to the foot, gives the opening 3.29 × 4.91 m under capitals 5.81 m up.

**Cause:** The core's first modelling (`20e9547`, 2026-10-11) gave the door a round head, 2.6 × 5.0 m, an estimate no source supports. The research index still listed #33, which the facade's code cites, as "Not viewed", so nothing recorded what it shows of the door. M2b's doors worker read #33 at full size and noted the discrepancy in the spec and the code, but left the head as it was, outside that milestone.

**Correction:** The hole, the frame and the leaves are square-headed (`doorUnit` rect, no fanlight), in a 0.19 m stone architrave (`surround`) under a plain stone frieze up to the porch's entablature, in place of the voussoir ring. The reveals and the head's soffit are lined in stone over a stone threshold, as #33 and fig. 110 show them. The opening is the 1888 drawing's scaled with the model's smaller porch: 2.83 × 4.23 m. The hole option `facets`, added for the arched frame, is gone (`src/model/core/weston-facade.ts`, `arches.ts`).

**Check:** No automated gate tells a modelled form from the one its photograph shows. The check is the visual audit the local rules ask of every modelling change: the shots read at native resolution beside the photographs they cite, here `doors-weston` and `preset-int-petrie-court` against #33 and fig. 110. A recorded reading would have caught this sooner: every image a model source cites marked viewed in `docs/research/`, with what it shows. Since work unit 4 (2026-10-28) that is a gate: `src/cited-images.test.ts` fails when a file under `src/model/` cites an entry of `interior-images.md` or `exterior-images.md` that is not marked viewed, the citations now in one form, `interior-images.md #33`. It went red with #33 put back to "Not viewed" (`docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`). A photograph cited without its index's name is outside it. The door's fit keeps its own gate: `src/model/door-fit.test.ts` goes red with the stone lining's opening 2 cm narrower or lower than the frame, and with the 528/529 wall left in the doorway.

**What it says about other checks:** A discrepancy a reviewer or worker notes but leaves out of scope needs a follow-up task with an owner, or it stays in the model as a note beside the code. The cited-but-unviewed images were where the next one of these was likeliest. Viewing them for the gate found one: the choir screen's tiers and cresting against #29 (work unit 4).

## The wheel stops zooming (2026-10-15)

**Reported symptom:** "scroll wheel doesn't always work so well. Sometimes it stops zooming at all, like it's hard capped on something."

**Investigation:** Real wheel notches (deltaY −100) driven by Playwright from four presets at t = N, with the camera-to-target distance logged after each notch (probe on `a3d077d`; controls `minDistance` 0.3 m, `maxDistance` 6000 m, `zoomToCursor` on, `zoomSpeed` 1; the rig's only clamp, the camera floor 0.4 m above the workspace ground, never fired):

- `aerial-ne`: 596 m to the target; each notch moved the camera 5.0 % of that distance (29.8 m, then 28.3 m, ...); by notch 100 a notch moved 0.19 m, by notch 140 it moved 2.4 cm, and at notch 150 it stopped dead at 0.300 m from the target. The camera stood at (−7.5, 15.0, −105.0), inside the building, below the core's roof: it had zoomed straight through the roof.
- `front`: the same decay, dead at notch 141, 0.300 m from the target, at (−7.5, 14.8, −104.7), again inside the building.
- `int-great-hall-floor-north`: 25 m to the target; dead at notch 88.
- `int-grand-stair-west`: 35 m to the target; dead at notch 94 at Z −64.8, 2 m short of the cross hall's back wall, which was still in plain view.
- From each stall, zooming out moved 1.6 cm per notch (5 % of 0.3 m), so the camera seemed stuck both ways.

**Cause:** OrbitControls' dolly scales the camera-to-target distance by 0.95 per notch and, with `zoomToCursor`, puts the target back in front of the camera at the new distance. The step is therefore 5 % of the distance to an invisible point that the camera carries along, not to anything under the pointer: it shrinks geometrically whatever is in view, ends at `minDistance` (0.3 m) wherever that point happens to be, passes through walls and roofs on the way, and starts the zoom out from 0.3 m. Neither the presets, the damping, the fit framing nor the camera floor was involved.

**Correction:** The controls' own zoom is off (`src/viewer/camera-rig.ts`). The wheel, a pinch and a middle-button drag step through `dollyStep` (`src/viewer/zoom.ts`) toward the surface under the pointer, found by `src/viewer/pick.ts` (the first drawn piece through per-geometry BVHs, or the workspace ground). Zooming in moves a tenth of the distance to that surface, at least 0.25 m, and stops 0.5 m before it; over open sky it flies on as if a surface stood 10 m ahead. Zooming out moves a tenth of the distance to the target, at least 0.25 m, until the target is 6 km away. Each step puts the target on the view axis at the depth it heads for.

**A second cause (2026-10-22, review of work unit 1, finding U8):** On 2026-10-15 one full e2e run found the after-W camera "notch 51 in moved the camera 0.029 m off the ray under the pointer", under load (its zoom tests ran about half again as long as usual), and no rerun showed it. Watching every wheel event and every frame from inside the page (`window.__met.camera` only) in 40 more runs, at 4× CPU throttle and with every CPU busy, the camera never moved between notches and each step lay on the ray within 1e-7 rad; a sweep of the W hold from 150 to 1600 ms then reproduced it at 500 and 700 ms. A W move that long carries the front preset's ray over the roofs and past the site, onto the workspace ground about 500 m away. Along that 9° ray the stop 0.5 m before the ground lies 8 cm over it, under the camera floor 0.4 m over it, so at notch 51 the floor lifted the camera 2.9 cm off the ray; from then on each notch slid it 0.25 m along the floor with the ground still 2.5 m ahead, and the zoom never reached a bound. In the failing run the 400 ms hold must have run long under that load. The zoom in now heads for the camera floor where its ray comes down to it before the stop (`zoomHit`, `src/viewer/zoom.ts`), and stops there on the ray.

**A third (2026-10-22, review of work unit 1, finding U11):** That floor bound let both bounds hold at once. Zoomed out to the far bound looking down, then held down by Q until the camera stood on the camera floor, the zoom in stopped at the floor and the zoom out at the far bound, so no notch moved the camera either way. And at the floor looking up, a zoom out backed down the ray and was lifted straight up again (`src/viewer/camera-rig.ts`): it moved only the step times the cosine of the ray's angle up (0.177 m at 45° for a target 1 m away), nothing looking straight up, and off the ray. Now a zoom out backing down a ray stops on the camera floor, on the ray, and where the far bound holds while the zoom in is at its near bound, a notch out first brings the target onto the view axis at the near bound's depth, so it moves (`zoomStep`).

**A fourth (2026-10-22, review 4, finding U12):** With the floor a bound of the zoom, nothing lifted a camera that a pan had taken under the floor before a notch, and the next frame lifted the camera alone, which turned the view toward its target: 96° after a right-drag and a notch in one frame with a surface just over the camera, 3° after the pan alone (that one older). Now every floor correction lifts the camera and its target together, and a zoom makes it before it takes its ray (`src/viewer/camera-rig.ts`). Its target stays on the view axis, so looking down from the floor it lies under the floor, which no longer turns anything.

**A fifth (2026-10-25, review 5, finding U13):** The floor correction undid itself. After the lift the rig ran the controls' update, which spent more of the pan a drag still owed and took the camera back under the floor before the frame rendered: 0.664 m under it (y −0.264 against 0.4) after the first frame of a 100-pixel pan up from the floor. The controls now take the lift through an update that spends nothing (damping at a factor of 0), so the lift is the last move before each render; the next frame spends what is owed and is lifted again (`src/viewer/camera-rig.ts`).

**Check:** `tests/e2e/zoom.spec.ts` covers the class "camera input stops responding at a limit". From `aerial-ne`, `front`, `int-grand-stair-west`, after a move by the W key and from over the park looking down toward the workspace ground beyond the site, it drives up to 120 real wheel notches in at the middle of the view (whole pixels). It finds the surface under the pointer apart from the page: the pointer's ray rebuilt from the published camera and the canvas as the page's projection casts it, then cast triangle by triangle through the finished model built in the test's own process, both faces counting, plus the workspace ground (`tests/e2e/surface.ts`; no BVH, no `src/viewer/pick.ts`, no orbit target). Every notch must keep the camera on that ray within 1 cm, never take it past the stop, 0.5 m before that surface or where the ray comes down to the camera floor if sooner, and move it at least min(0.25 m, what is left to the stop) − 1 mm until it is there. There a notch in must move nothing, and each of 8 notches out must move at least 0.25 m. From `aerial-ne` it also drives the wheel out to the 6 km bound, where a notch in must move again; from there Q held lowers the camera onto the camera floor, where a notch in must move nothing and a notch out must move, and the zoom in and out must hold as above. Looking up and lowered onto the floor by Q, a notch out must move nothing, a notch in must move at least 0.25 m along the ray, and the next notch out must come back onto the floor on the ray. Under a footing on the floor, a right-drag that pans the camera under the floor and a notch sent at once must leave the view as it was. `src/viewer/zoom.test.ts` checks the same step rules on numbers, and `src/viewer/camera-floor.test.ts` the drag and the notch in one frame through the rig's handlers, and that at every frame while a drag's damping runs out (a pan, a pan with W held, an orbit) the camera the frame renders from stands on or over the floor, a pan's view unturned. Until 2026-10-15 the gate read what was left to the stop from the camera-to-target distance, which the zoom itself sets, so a picker returning only that distance passed while the aerial camera stopped 21.6 m beyond the first surface (review of work unit 1, finding U5). On 2026-10-15 the gate briefly took its line from the first notch's move, within 2 mrad of the rebuilt ray, to pass the after-W miss; that passed a ray biased 1.5 mrad down, whose stop from `front` is 43 cm short (finding U8), and the rebuilt ray is the oracle again.

**Gate proof:** With OrbitControls' zoom put back (`enableZoom`, `zoomToCursor`, `minDistance` 0.3, the new zoom input off), `zoom.spec.ts` went red on 5 of its 6 tests. For example, `aerial-ne`: "notch 95 in moved 0.2402 m with 4.303 m left to the bound". The far-bound test failed too. The sixth test, a preset chosen after zooming, passes either way. With the fix, all 6 pass. Against the independent surface (2026-10-15), a picker that returns the camera-to-target distance turns 4 of the 6 tests red: from `aerial-ne` "notch 32 in put the camera 1.648 m past its stop, 0.5 m before the first surface 574.754 m along the ray under the pointer", from `front` 7.661 m past it at notch 14, and from `int-grand-stair-west` and after W the zoom stalls 1.706 m and 63.405 m short of the stop; the far-bound and preset tests pass either way, and with the scene pick all 6 pass. With the camera floor as a bound (2026-10-22), 7 tests: without `zoomHit` the case toward the workspace ground goes red, "notch 43 in moved the camera 0.022 m off the ray under the pointer"; the camera-to-target picker turns 5 red (as above, and the ground case stalls 267.730 m short); a ray biased 1.5 mrad down turns 5 red at notch 1 or 2, from 1.0 cm (`int-grand-stair-west`) to 8.6 cm (`aerial-ne`) off the ray; with all three restored all 7 pass. The two Q-held cases (2026-10-22), against the zoom of `8a5f651`: from the far bound on the floor "a notch out from both bounds at once" moved 0 m; looking up, "a notch out on the floor" moved 1.110 m, lifted off the ray; with `zoomStep` all 9 tests pass. The drag and notch under a footing (U12) turned the view 7.0° at `29c93d3`; `src/viewer/camera-floor.test.ts` turns 95.8° and 3.0° there; with the floor correction all pass. With the rig of `0fd3607` (U13), its three per-frame cases go red: the pan leaves the camera under the floor in 84 frames, at most 0.204 m, with W held in 59 (0.145 m), the orbit in 13 (1.116 m); with the lift taken without spending, all pass, and spending all of it at the floor instead, which stops the drag there, puts the camera 5.03 m off the drag's path in the first frame.

**What it says about other checks:** Before this, no e2e test drove the wheel more than a few notches, and none checked that an input keeps working near a limit. A camera input with a clamp or a multiplicative step can reach that limit after enough use, so any new input (keys, touch) needs a gate that drives it to its bound and back.
