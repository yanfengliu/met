# The Medieval Sculpture Hall's end windows measured

## Problem and outcome

Work unit 4's F-owner-6 (`docs/work/4_cited-images-viewed/plan.md`, from its review 5) says the hall's round end windows may be too high and too small. From the preset that stands for P8's view (`int-medieval-hall-east`), the model's east window shows above the choir screen's middle frieze, where P8 shows it a third of the way down the middle tier. And if #29 (the Met's MM29152) was taken from the east balcony, its west window is about 3.3 m of glazing in a 5 m surround, against the model's 2.4 m. The model builds both windows 2.4 m across, centred at `VAUX_OCULUS_Y` (21.4 m, 17.29 m over the hall floor, an estimate). Nobody had measured them.

Outcome: where #29 was taken from settled, each window's height and size measured on P8 and #29 at native resolution with its uncertainty, the method and numbers in `docs/research/interior.md`, and the model's windows moved and resized to match, or the reason they are not recorded.

## Scope

Included: P8 and #29 read at native resolution in the browser and measured on a canvas; the Met's floor map (`interior-images.md` #34) for the plan positions the photographs need; `docs/research/interior.md` and the two photographs' entries in `interior-images.md`; the oculi in the model: `VAUX_OCULUS_Y` and the windows' size (`src/model/core/constants.ts`), the gable wall's hole (`vauxHall`, `vaux-shell.ts`), the glass discs (`westFrontWindows`, `vaux-front.ts`) and the plaster lining's hole (`endWallLinings`, `medieval-hall.ts`).

Excluded: the rest of those files, which other sessions are changing (the 1880 fronts, the Weston porch). Also excluded, and reported to the owner as findings, are the choir screen's place along the hall (`SCREEN_Z`), the preset that stands for P8, the end doorways and the windows' surround. The first three are needed to read the photographs and disagree with the evidence; the last is measured but not built (see Findings).

## Approach

The screen gives each photograph's scale at the screen's plane (12.80 × 15.85 m, the friezes' heights measured on #29 in work unit 4). A window stands on an end wall behind the screen, so its size and height also need the camera's distances to the screen and to that wall. Each photograph's camera is placed from what it shows, with the Met's floor map giving where the screen, the end walls and their doorways stand:

- P8 (Nikon Coolpix P5000 at 7.5 mm, so f ≈ 2,700 px). The pitch comes from the balusters' vanishing point, fitted on positions corrected for the lens's barrel distortion at the value where the two tiers agree; the friezes' edges give a second calibration of the distortion. The camera's distance to the screen and its eye height come from the friezes' heights. A man at the far end gives a weak check of the pitch.
- #29 (a view camera, nearly level, its view shifted up). It is solved as one pinhole camera from the cross's height, the balusters' vanishing point, the two floor lines and the hall's east doorway, which frames it (2.28 m on the map). The west doorway checks it.

Each window's surround and glazing edges are then measured as circles and converted at its wall's distance. One script computes every number from the readings, and it is kept with this unit (`snapshots/windows.py.txt`). The two windows are compared, and the model takes their weighted average, rounded.

## Findings for the owner

- F-owner-1, the choir screen's place along the hall. The Met's floor map draws a partition across the nave at Z −106.12 (world frame), from the piers' centres to 1.15 m either side of the axis. Its 2.3 m gap is the gate, so this is the screen, standing on the third pier pair from the west. The model has `SCREEN_Z` −99.4, 6.7 m further east, near the middle pair, an estimate made from P8's perspective. The photographs agree with the map. Read with the screen at the map's place, the hall's two end windows, a symmetric pair, measure alike: 16.8 m (east, P8) and 16.0 m (west, #29) over the floor. Read with it at the model's place, they would measure 13.9 and 22.0 m. That places the screen only roughly: the two readings would meet exactly with it 0.7 m east of the map's line. #29's west doorway also reads as wide as the map draws it at the map's place (277 to 285 px predicted, 287 px read between its jambs' inner edges), where at the model's place it would read 202 to 208 px. That check rests on the map's doorway width: doorways 3.05 m wide would fit the model's place, though #29's west window would still read 21.3 m. P8 alone gives only its camera's distance from the screen (12.58 m), not the place. Moving the screen changes the walk tests at the gate (`choir-gate.test.ts`), the collection's clearance round it (`placement.test.ts`) and the hall's views, so it is the owner's call. Until then the model's screen stands 6.7 m east of the photographs'.
- F-owner-2, the preset that stands for P8. `int-medieval-hall-east` puts the camera at Z −112.9 with a 62° field. P8's vertical field is about 53° once its lens's distortion is undone (51.3° as found, from the camera's 36 mm equivalent), its eye 1.59 m up and pitched 22.0° up, 12.58 m from the screen. If F-owner-1 holds, P8 was taken from about Z −118.8, the hall's west doorway, and from the preset the screen and the windows can never sit where P8 shows them while the screen stands at −99.4. If the screen stays where it is, P8's camera would stand at −112.1, 0.8 m east of the preset's −112.9, and the preset needs that move as well as its field and pitch: from there the moved east window falls at 7.98 m on the screen's west face, against 8.21 m from the preset. With the screen at the map's place, the window measured here falls a third of the way down the middle tier from P8's camera, as in P8, which is how it was measured, so that is no independent check.
- F-owner-3, the end doorways. The map draws each end doorway 2.28 m wide, and #29 shows the east one as a round arch over that opening, springing about 3.9 m and crowned about 5.0 m over the floor, and reads the west one 287 px between its jambs, 2.29 m with the screen at the map's place; P8 shows the east one about 2.3 m wide inside a broader moulded archivolt. The model's `END_DOOR` (`src/model/core/vaux-shell.ts`) is 4.4 m wide and 5.9 m tall, an estimate from P8, and it also sizes the plaster archivolt (`medieval-hall.ts`). Not changed here: it lies outside the oculi, in a file another session is changing.
- F-owner-4, the windows' surround. Both photographs show each window's glazing in a round, splayed plaster reveal about 3.4 m across at the wall's inner face, the glazing set back 0.37 to 0.58 m. The model builds only the 2.25 m opening, whose own reveal shows the gable wall's brick inside the hall. Building the splay means a plaster cone inside that brick wall, with brick outside it: the wall's hole widened and the cone and a brick ring fitted into it face to face, or the 1.1 m wall split in two layers. Either restructures the gable wall's geometry, so it is left for the owner's call. The core area's mesh budget is not the obstacle: merged into the existing geometries, the cone and ring add triangles, not meshes.
